# Reference: "this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS" (Jules, 19/10/2026)

The lead magnet Jules points to as "it worked well". Visual: `own-ats-post-visual.png` (image) and `own-ats-video-frame.png` (one frame of the video posted with it).

## The 5 hooks proposed (the author picked #1)
1) [Authority] this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS. here's how. ✅ picked (strong number + promise of a mechanism)
2) [Contrarian] stop renting an ATS built for every firm. the best agencies build their own.
3) [Pain] you pay for 100% of your ATS. your team uses 20% of it.
4) [Curiosity] building an ATS used to take years. this agency did it in weeks.
5) [The named gap] every agency complains about its ATS. the best ones fixed it with ONE decision.

## The post
this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS. here's how.
(and it's a big part of how they're hitting $50M this year)
they stopped renting a tool built for every firm. paying for 100%, using maybe 20%.
so they built their own, around how they work.
here's what's inside:
1/ clients & jobs. every client, every open job, the sales pipeline
2/ candidates. profiles, stages, CVs read automatically
3/ notes & calls. every call summarized straight into the profile
4/ email & calendar. everything synced on its own
5/ outreach. email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, follow-ups (hard part #1, so they plugged it)
6/ sourcing. a job is created, a ranked shortlist appears (hard part #2, plugged too)
7/ reporting. results per recruiter and per client
and moving from the old ATS takes 6 steps: map, export, clean, build, test, switch.
building an ATS used to take years and a big team.
today you build the easy parts yourself, and plug agents for the 2 hard ones: sourcing and outreach.
I put the whole thing in one guide, step by step (every part, what it takes, how to move over).
it's yours.

## Why it worked (what to copy, line by line)
| Line | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| "this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS. here's how." | Proof (a precise number) + the object named in line 1 + a promise of method. |
| "(and it's a big part of how they're hitting $50M this year)" | The reveal right after the hook, in parentheses. Raises the stakes. |
| "paying for 100%, using maybe 20%" | The small pain, in one line, that every reader lives. |
| "1/ … 7/" | Gives EVERYTHING in the post. The reader learns something even without commenting. |
| "(hard part #1, so they plugged it)" | Places the product naturally, as part of the method, never as a pitch. |
| "6 steps: map, export, clean, build, test, switch" | A second piece of value, short, that makes the guide feel big. |
| "I put the whole thing in one guide … it's yours." | The soft line. Zero instruction. The keyword ATS is in the visual. |
