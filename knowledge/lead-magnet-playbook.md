# The lead magnet playbook

From Jules Granier (CEO of Kalent). This is the recipe behind his lead magnets on LinkedIn. The last one ("this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS") is in `examples/own-ats-post.md`, read it first.

## 1. What a lead magnet is for

A normal post gets likes. A lead magnet gets **conversations with people who raised their hand**. Everyone who comments the keyword is a lead who told you, in public, "I have this problem". You DM them the resource, you qualify them, the best ones get a call.

The whole game fits in one line: **give enough to get a "wow", hold back enough that they need you for the rest.**

## 2. Wow + frustration, the core mechanic

| | What it looks like | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Wow** | The post and the visual really give: the mechanism, the steps, a real number, a real example. The reader can act on it today. | If the free part is empty, nobody comments. "Teasing a closed box" is the format that converts the least on LinkedIn. |
| **Frustration** | The last part is visibly there but locked: blurred pages, "step 4 and 5 in the full playbook", "4 more states in your full report". | The reader sees exactly what they are missing. The gap is what makes them comment, then book. |
| **Access** | The keyword to comment is **in the image or video**, never in the text. In the text, one soft line: "I put the whole thing in one guide. it's yours." | LinkedIn reads the text and cuts the reach of posts that ask for comments. It does not read the image. |
| **Demo** | The resource itself ends on what only you can do: "we run this on your real data in 15 minutes". | The resource shows the method. The demo applies it to THEM. That's the reason to book. |

The order never changes: **give the info, let the gap appear, then give access.** Access must feel like the logical next step, never like a toll.

## 3. A lead magnet is 3 deliverables, not a post

1. **The resource** (guide, checklist, calculator, template, free tool). It exists BEFORE the post goes out: comments arrive in the first hour.
2. **The post**: hook + real value + soft line. 5 hooks proposed, the author picks.
3. **The visual**: image (FOMO preview), PDF carousel, or fast video. The keyword lives here.

## 4. What makes a good resource

- Something you open and **use in 10 minutes**, with a before and an after. A checklist you run, a calculator, a template you duplicate, a teardown with screenshots.
- ⛔ A list of tips is not a resource. You read it once and never open it again.
- ⛔ A 40-page ebook is not a resource. Nobody opens it.
- The best ones come from **what you already explain by hand** on every call. That is why the author must bring the material (section 7).

### The free tool, the strongest format
A free tool (like the nexus check in `tools/examples/`) is a lead magnet that works on the reader's own numbers:
- **Wow**: a big, personal number in 30 seconds ("6 states where you likely owe sales tax").
- **Frustration**: the first 2 results in full, the rest blurred.
- **Demo**: "book 15 minutes, we check your real data". The click goes straight to the calendar.
Jules built one for Kalent: you describe a role, the agent finds the 5 best profiles live, the rest is behind a call.

## 5. The hook does 90% of the work

- LinkedIn cuts after ~200 characters. The hook must work alone.
- **A hook states a fact.** It is controversial, relatable or valuable. Never a soft question, never a vague promise.
- **The name of the object is in the first line.** "this $50M staffing agency built its own ATS" names the object (an ATS) and the proof ($50M).
- **For a lead magnet, the hook involves the reader** ("you can find every state you owe tax in, in 10 minutes"), or names a result they want.
- **Clarity first**: if someone who doesn't know you has to read it twice, it's dead. Say what IS, in the present, in simple words.
- Always 5 hooks from 5 different templates (see `hooks.md`). The author picks.

## 6. Picking the visual

| Format | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Image** (`post-image.png`) | Default. Fastest to make. | Title = the hook. 3 cards: 1 readable, 2 blurred + "🔒 Full playbook". Black band at the bottom: "Comment to get access" + KEYWORD, huge, readable on a phone. |
| **PDF carousel** | The value is a sequence of steps. LinkedIn document posts keep people swiping (dwell time). | Cover = hook. 2-4 slides that really give. 1 locked slide. Last slide = keyword. |
| **Video** | You have a product to show, or a number that hits. Highest reach when it's good. | Fast: a cut every 1.5-3 s, something moves in every frame, the keyword band stays on screen the whole time. 12-20 s. Show the real product (screenshots), never stock footage. |

The keyword is **distinctive** (NEXUS, ATS, AUDIT), never a common word, so you can filter comments.

## 7. The author brings the context. Always.

A lead magnet written without the author's material is generic, and generic gets zero comments. The kit refuses to write before it has:
1. **The pain, in your customers' words**: a scene from a real call ("we got a letter from Washington asking for 3 years of back taxes").
2. **The object**: what you explain by hand on every call, or the spreadsheet your team uses.
3. **The proof**: a real number or a real (anonymous) case.
4. **The inspiration**: 1 to 3 posts that went viral that you like (format, hook, visual). We never start from a blank page: we take what already worked and adapt the mechanism.
5. **The next step**: what the demo gives them that the resource doesn't.

## 8. The flow after the post (where the pipeline is made)

1. **Comment** → reply to every comment in the first hour (it boosts the post) and send the resource in DM.
2. **Send the resource automatically** if volume is high (a scraper pulls the commenters, a tool sends the DM).
3. **Qualify**: company, role, size, fit. Enrich the best ones (work email, phone), only with a go.
4. **Warm campaign** for the best ones: a short email + LinkedIn sequence that starts from the resource ("you grabbed the nexus checklist, did step 2 surprise you?").
5. **Nurture** the others: they get the next lead magnet and the case study.
6. **Measure**: not likes. In order: demos booked, DMs, keyword comments from the target, who commented. A post at 400 reactions and zero target comments is a bad post.

## 9. Rhythm

- 2 lead magnets a week is the pace that works. One resource can feed several posts (different hooks, different formats).
- Never two posts in a row with the same hook type, same length, same format.
- A resource that worked becomes: an image post, then a carousel, then a video, then the first email of a campaign.
